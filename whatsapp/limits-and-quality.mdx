Meta caps how many customers your number may start a conversation with in a rolling 24 hours, and grades how those messages are received. Both belong to Meta — Watx reads them and shows them where they matter.

## Messaging tiers

| Tier | Shown as | Conversations per 24 hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TIER_50` | **Restricted — 50/day** | 50 |
| `TIER_250` | **Unverified — 250/day** | 250 |
| `TIER_1K` | **1K/day** | 1,000 |
| `TIER_2K` | **Tier 1 — 2K/day** | 2,000 |
| `TIER_10K` | **Tier 2 — 10K/day** | 10,000 |
| `TIER_100K` | **Tier 3 — 100K/day** | 100,000 |
| `TIER_UNLIMITED` | **Unlimited** | No daily cap |

A number that has never been synced reads **Not synced yet**, and **Refresh** fetches it. A number Meta has answered for but not graded reads **Not reported by Meta**: Refresh changes nothing there, because Meta assigns the tier itself, usually after business verification or your first campaigns, and new business portfolios can reach 250 unique customers a day in the meantime. If Meta ever reports a tier Watx does not recognise, it is shown raw and usage tracking pauses rather than guessing a ceiling.

An unverified number is capped at 250 conversations a day; verifying your business in Meta Business Manager is what moves it up a tier.

## Quality rating

Meta grades the number from how recipients react — blocks, reports and "not useful" feedback.

| Rating | Shown as |
| --- | --- |
| `GREEN` | Good quality |
| `YELLOW` | Medium quality |
| `RED` | Low quality |
| `NA` | Not yet rated — the normal state for a new number |

<Warning>
  At **Low quality**, Meta may throttle or pause delivery. Watx says so and advises against bulk sends until it recovers. A template can also be paused on its own at Meta for the same reason — see [Message templates](/whatsapp/templates).
</Warning>

## Where it is shown

Two places, both WhatsApp-only:

- **Analytics → Sending capacity** — the tier, the rating, the usage meter and any warning banners.
- **Broadcast step 4**, above the Send button, as a pre-flight check.

The quality rating on its own also appears on the connected card in [channel settings](/whatsapp/settings).

## The usage meter

The meter counts **distinct contacts** you have messaged in the last 24 hours, because Meta's cap is on unique customers, not messages.

<Warning>
  **It counts broadcast recipients only.** Messages from automations, flows and the inbox consume the same Meta quota and are not counted here, so the number is a floor, not a total. That caveat is printed under the meter rather than hidden in a tooltip, and the pre-flight warning repeats it.
</Warning>

The meter never blocks a send. An undercounting number must not be allowed to stop a legitimate broadcast.

## Refreshing

Watx refreshes the tier and quality rating from Meta every six hours, and **Refresh** on the card asks for an immediate update. Data older than twelve hours is labelled **may be out of date**.

Two things stop the refresh without stopping your messages:

- **The access token expired.** Limits stop updating; sending still works. Reconnect from [channel settings](/whatsapp/settings).
- **Meta was unreachable or rate-limited.** The last known values stay on screen and are marked stale rather than being wiped.

## Pre-flight warning on a broadcast

When an audience looks bigger than your remaining daily allowance, step 4 of the broadcast wizard says so before you press Send: messages past the limit are rejected by Meta. It is a warning, not a block — see above for why.

## Send pacing

Broadcasts are paced at roughly 10 messages a second by default, which matches the tier a new number starts on. That pacing is global to the deployment rather than per number, so a very large broadcast can slow another one down slightly; it exists so no single number ever exceeds Meta's rate.
